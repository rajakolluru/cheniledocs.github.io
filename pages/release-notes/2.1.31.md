## Highlights

### Annotation-based services, decoupled from the REST controller

You can now define a full Chenile service by annotating a **plain Spring bean** with `@ChenileController` (and `@ChenileOperation` on its methods) — with **no `@RestController`, no `ControllerSupport`, and no hand-written `@PostMapping`**. `chenile-core`'s `AnnotationChenileServiceInitializer` registers these non-HTTP services; `chenile-http` continues to handle REST controllers. HTTP exposure is now an opt-in layer rather than a prerequisite for defining a service.

→ [Annotation-based Chenile services]({{ '/concepts/annotation-based-services/' | relative_url }})

### Optional service-registry registration

`@ChenileController` gains a `registerInServiceRegistry` flag (default `true`). Set it to `false` to define a service that is fully wired into the runtime but **not** published into `chenile-service-registry` — ideal for internal-only helpers, mocks, and services never reached through a remote proxy.

→ [Annotation-based Chenile services]({{ '/concepts/annotation-based-services/' | relative_url }})

## Upgrade notes

Once published, point your application's parent (or `chenile.*.version` properties) at `2.1.31` and rebuild — the 11 repositories move together under `chenile-parent`.

```xml
<parent>
  <groupId>org.chenile</groupId>
  <artifactId>chenile-parent</artifactId>
  <version>2.1.31</version>
</parent>
```
